# Duel Monsters MTG — opponent roster & decks

Separate from `docs/DECKLISTS.md` (that's Product 1). All 16 P2 opponents have
**creatures-only** decks built from our MTG creature pool.

**Opponents summon monsters only** — no magic/equip/field card ever appears in a
deck. Decks are authored in `work/duelmonsters-mtg/deck_config.json` as `{pool: {card_id: weight}}`,
normalized to the engine's 2048 total and written to bank 8 by `build.py` via
`decks.py` (which refuses any non-monster). `mtg_decks.py` generates a deck from a
colour set + a **target weighted-average ATK**, band-selecting creatures around that
target and weighting them gently, so each deck plays as a real curve.

## Progression is 4 / 9 / 3 (verified in code)

`docs/NOTES.md` → "Opponent progression". The opponent index `$CEEF` has six
writers: a group of **4 (slots 9–12, via `add $09`)**, a group of **9 (slots 0–8)**,
and **3 fixed bosses (13/14/15)**. Stock slots 9–12 are the friends you duel first,
so the flow is **4 → 9 → 3**. (Call-site addresses do NOT give the order — the stock
roster does.) Difficulty must ascend across those stages; slot id is only a data index.

## Design notes

- **Stage 1 (4)** — the novices: Sindbad and Ali Baba even run their own namesake
  cards from Arabian Nights as signatures.
- **Stage 2 (9)** — four master mages (Serra, Ashnod, Tawnos, Teferi) then the
  **five Elder Dragons**, each running their **three-colour wedge** with their own
  4000/4000 card pinned at ~11% of the deck. Every dragon lands below the weakest boss.
- **Bosses (3)** — hand-built for flavour: Mishra (red war-machines, Mishra's War
  Machine 9.3%), Urza (white knights + refined constructs, Akron Legionnaire/Urza's
  Avenger), Yawgmoth (black demons + Phyrexian artifacts, Yawgmoth Demon 9.8%).
- Signature picks: Serra→Serra Angel, Teferi→Mahamoti Djinn, Ashnod→Su-Chi (she
  built it), Tawnos→Bronze Horse, each Elder Dragon→itself.

## Win-count rewards — all 16 pools built

Beating a duelist 10/20/…/100 times awards a card (thresholds unchanged from
stock). Rule: **each opponent's 10 rewards are the 10 best cards in their own
deck**, where "best" is `max(ATK, DEF)` — a high-DEF body counts exactly as much
as a high-ATK one, so walls and defensive giants are first-class rewards
(Obsianus Golem 1600/2400 ranks as 2400; Ghost Ship 1100/1600 as 1600).

Ordered **weakest at 10 wins → best at 100 wins**, so the grind escalates. Each
Elder Dragon therefore awards **itself** (4000/4000) at 100 wins, and each boss
awards their own signature bomb. Generated into `work/duelmonsters-mtg/reward_config.json` from
`deck_config.json` and applied by `build.py` (160 values written).

Example — Yawgmoth (pool 4): Mishra's War Machine → Juggernaut → Ebon Praetor →
Mold Demon → Nightmare → Obsianus Golem → **Yawgmoth Demon** → Cosmic Horror →
Lord of the Pit → **Colossus of Sardia**.

> Note: Colossus of Sardia (3750/3600) is the 100-win reward for Tawnos, Mishra,
> Urza *and* Yawgmoth, because it sits in all four artifact decks. That follows
> the rule as specified; trim it from the non-boss decks if the repetition (or a
> mid-tier opponent handing out the best artifact) is unwanted.


## Leviathan — the bosses' answer to the Elder Dragons

Once the player can field 4000/4000 dragons, the three bosses need something that
stands up to them. **Leviathan (#194, Blue 10/10 -> 4000/4000, The Dark)** was added
to Mishra's, Urza's and Yawgmoth's decks at ~5% each. The ladder still ascends:
Nicol Bolas 1885 -> Mishra 2125 -> Urza 2189 -> Yawgmoth 2416. It also becomes each
boss's 100-win reward (it is now the best card in their deck), pushing Colossus of
Sardia to 90 wins.

## Drop pools — all 16 built, full coverage

Generated by `work/scripts/mtg_drops.py` into `work/duelmonsters-mtg/drop_config.json`, applied by
`build.py` through `drops.py` (which gained an explicit `"pools"` config shape,
mirroring `deck_config.json`; Duel Monsters Kaizo's mode/boost path is untouched).

1. **You drop what you play** — every opponent drops their own deck's cards, with
   weight falling as power rises (`w = 100 / (1 + value/500)`, value = `max(ATK, DEF)`),
   so grinding a duelist builds toward their archetype. A 400-power common is ~5x
   likelier than a 4000-power bomb.
2. **Yawgmoth drops every Elder Dragon** — all five at 2.25% each (~11% chance per
   win of landing *some* dragon). He is the only source of the 4000/4000 apex cards.
3. **Full coverage** — every remaining card is assigned by **colour** and **power
   tier** (weak cards from the earliest opponent of that colour, strong from the
   latest). Verified in the built ROM: **365/365 cards are droppable**, every pool
   totalling exactly 2048.

Unlike decks, drop pools DO include non-monsters — spells, equips and the six
lands are only obtainable this way.

## Regenerating everything

Card ids shift whenever the roster changes, so no config stores ids by hand — the
three hand-built boss decks are declared **by card name** in `mtg_gen.py` and
resolved at generation time. The pipeline is:

```
python mtg_assemble.py            # creatures + spells + tokens -> cards.json
python mtg_gen.py                 # decks, rewards, equips, drops, starter, fusions
python build.py --product duelmonsters-mtg    # -> build/duelmonsters-mtg-hack.gb
```

`work/duelmonsters-mtg/_cuts.json` holds the retired creature names, applied by `mtg_assemble.py`.

## Fusions — a same-colour upgrade ladder

MTG has no fusion, so Duel Monsters MTG invents one rule the player can actually learn:

> **Two creatures of the same colour fuse into a stronger creature of that colour,
> with roughly their combined power.**

400+400 makes an ~800 body, 800+800 makes ~1600, and two 2000+ bodies of a colour
reach that colour's **Elder Dragon** (4000/4000). Colourless tops out at Colossus
of Sardia. Every colour is therefore a self-contained upgrade ladder, and all six
capstones are reachable (Arcades ×43, Chromium ×50, Bolas ×56, Palladia ×55,
Vaevictis ×45, Colossus ×25).

Generated by `mtg_fusions.py`. The table is a fixed **2159 rows** (the count is a
hardcoded immediate in three places), so every row holds a real recipe with a
**distinct material pair** — a duplicate pair would be a dead row, since the
engine takes the first match. Verified in the built ROM:

- 2159/2159 rows same-colour, all materials and results inside #1–300
- 2159 distinct material pairs (no dead rows)
- result always stronger than both materials; **0 ATK-downgrades, 0 wall results**
  (a 0-ATK body is barred from being a fusion *result*, so fusing two attackers
  can never hand you a wall)

## Starter pool — 100 cards

Table at **0x26AC**, exactly **100 × 16-bit card ids**, immediately followed by
code, so it is a hard 100 and cannot grow (`starter.py`, config
`work/duelmonsters-mtg/starter_config.json`).

Stock was 100 monsters averaging 942 ATK. Ours is **90 creatures + 10 spells**:
15 low-power creatures of each colour (400–1200, avg 551) so any colour is
playable from turn one, plus **all six basic lands** — without them the
land/colour pump, the core mechanic, would be invisible until the first lucky
drop — and Lightning Bolt, Healing Salve, Holy Strength, Giant Growth.

> Stock's starter pool contains no magic cards at all. Including ten is the one
> choice here not backed by the original data; card ids are card ids, so it should
> be fine, but it is the first thing to revert if the opening deck misbehaves.

## Duel messages — spells now announce themselves

**What a card does and what it says are separate tables.** The verb tables run the
effect; `0x15162` + the pool at `0x15400` decide the printed text. So every
reskinned spell kept announcing its DM1 predecessor — Psionic Blast printed *"Used
Hinotama"* (caught in playtest).

Fixed by `messages.py` + `work/duelmonsters-mtg/message_config.json`, which repacks the
1726-byte pool and rewrites the 24 message ids our spells use:

- burns, heals, fields, Wrath, Terror, Shatter, Siren's Call, Festival, Marsh Gas
  and Amnesia each get their own correct text
- the six field messages now name the **colour** they pump ("Field now Forest. /
  Green grows stronger.")
- **all 26 equips share message id `0x10`**, so it uses neutral wording —
  *"Equipped a card. Monster powered up."* — rather than naming the wrong card

Pool usage 1574/1726 bytes. This config is static (message ids and spell slots are
fixed), so it does not need regenerating when the roster changes.

## Opponent identity — names, intros, defeat lines

Renaming opponents needs **three** separate edits (see `docs/NOTES.md` →
"Opponent text"); doing only one leaves stock characters showing:

1. **`duelist_config.json`** → the 8-byte name table. This is what the **record
   page** and duel HUD read. Names are capped at 8 tiles, hence `Vaevicts` and
   `Bolas`.
2. **`dialogue_config.json`** → **65 strings in two blocks**: the 17 intro
   speeches ("I am Teferi, mage of Zhalfir...") and the 48 in-duel lines (a
   pre-duel taunt, a victory line and a defeat line for each of the 16). Every
   archetype reference is reskinned — Weevil's *"My Insect Army will fill the
   field!"* is now Serra's *"My angel host takes wing!"*, Rex's dinosaurs become
   Tawnos's clockwork constructs, Mako's ocean becomes Teferi's control of time,
   and Keith's *"Card Professor tactics"* become Bolas's *"perfect Grixis
   tactics"*.
3. **`patches.json`** text entries → the handful of post-duel lines that name
   someone: *Pegasus defeated* → *Urza defeated*, *Bandit Keith* → *Nicol Bolas*,
   *M-my Blue-Eyes* → *M-my dragons*, and the title line *Yu-Gi-Oh! Duel
   Monsters!* → *Magic: The Gathering!*

Verified: **no stock character name remains anywhere in the dialogue banks.** The
translation credits at `0xF4900`+ are deliberately untouched.

## Starter pool — toned down

Playtest said the opening monsters were too strong. The cause was the thin
Colourless pool: only 30 artifacts exist, so its "weakest 15" reached up to
1200/1200 (Primal Clay, Coal Golem) while other colours contributed all-400s, and
a max-stat ranking also let 0/1600 walls in — brutal early blockers.

Now every starter creature must satisfy **300 ≤ ATK ≤ 800 and ATK+DEF ≤ 1600**,
ranked by total stats. Result: max ATK **800** (was 1200), max DEF **1200** (was
1600), no 0-ATK walls at all. Colours end up uneven (White 21 … Colourless 7)
because eligibility, not quota, now decides — which is the right trade.
